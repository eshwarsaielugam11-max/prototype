# Speech-Language Pathology and Evidence-Based Voice Therapies

When an individual experiences speech or voice changes associated with Parkinson's disease, proactive consultation with a licensed speech-language pathologist (SLP) provides meaningful therapeutic avenues to preserve vocal strength, improve articulation, and maintain communicative confidence.

Unlike general vocal exercises, evidence-based speech therapy protocols for Parkinson's target the specific neuromuscular and sensory processing deficits underlying hypokinetic dysarthria. Two prominent, scientifically validated clinical interventions include:

- **LSVT LOUD® (Lee Silverman Voice Treatment):** An intensive, standardized behavioral speech treatment focused on a single motor goal: vocal loudness ("Think Loud"). Delivered in sixteen one-hour sessions over four weeks, LSVT LOUD uses high-effort vocal exercises to stimulate respiratory drive and laryngeal muscle adduction. A crucial component of this protocol is sensory recalibration, teaching patients to recognize that speaking with greater vocal volume is appropriate and necessary to be heard comfortably by others.
- **SPEAK OUT!®:** Developed by the Parkinson Voice Project, this protocol focuses on converting speech from an automatic, basal-ganglia-mediated motor task into an intentional, conscious act ("Speak with Intent"). By recruiting cortical motor planning networks that remain relatively intact in early-to-mid stage Parkinson's, patients learn to overcome motor hesitation, improve vocal projection, and speak with sustained clarity.

Complementary interventions include Expiratory Muscle Strength Training (EMST)—utilizing a calibrated pressure threshold device to strengthen respiratory and cough muscles—and assistive communication technologies, such as personal voice amplifiers. Early referral to an SLP, even when speech changes are subtle, empowers individuals with foundational strategies that help sustain long-term conversational engagement.
