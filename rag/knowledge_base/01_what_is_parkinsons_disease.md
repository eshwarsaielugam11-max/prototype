# Understanding Parkinson's Disease: Neurological Overview

Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurological disorder that impacts the central nervous system, predominantly affecting motor function and physical coordination. The condition develops gradually over time as specific nerve cells within the brain experience degeneration, functional impairment, and loss.

At the neurochemical level, the primary motor manifestations of Parkinson's disease arise from the loss of specialized neurons located in a deep brain structure known as the substantia nigra. Under healthy conditions, these neurons synthesize dopamine, a vital neurotransmitter responsible for transmitting electrical and chemical instructions across neural circuits that modulate smooth, purposeful physical movements. When dopamine concentrations drop substantially due to neuronal depletion, the brain's ability to coordinate muscle activity becomes increasingly compromised.

Parkinson's disease is traditionally recognized through several cardinal motor indicators:
- **Resting Tremor:** An involuntary, rhythmic oscillation typically starting in one hand, finger, or limb while muscles are relaxed.
- **Bradykinesia:** Generalized slowness of voluntary movement, which can cause difficulty initiating actions, reduced stride length when walking, or diminished facial expression.
- **Muscle Rigidity:** Persistent stiffness or involuntary resistance in the limbs and neck that can restrict natural flexibility and cause physical discomfort.
- **Postural Instability:** Impairments in balance and equilibrium that tend to manifest as the condition advances, elevating the risk of accidental falls.

Beyond motor signs, individuals frequently experience non-motor symptoms, including sleep disturbances, olfactory changes, autonomic alterations, fatigue, and cognitive shifts. Because motor control extends throughout the vocal tract—involving the diaphragm, larynx, pharynx, tongue, and lips—subtle alterations in speech production and vocal dynamics frequently accompany these systemic neurological changes.
