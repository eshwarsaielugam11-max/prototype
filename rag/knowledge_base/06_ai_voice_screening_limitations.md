# Technical and Clinical Limitations of Acoustic AI Screening

While artificial intelligence offers promising analytical capabilities in computational acoustics, it is essential to understand that an AI voice screening tool is fundamentally not a diagnostic instrument. It cannot determine whether an individual has Parkinson's disease, and its outputs must always be interpreted in the context of inherent technical and clinical limitations.

Major confounding factors and limitations include:
- **Non-Specificity of Acoustic Features:** Acoustic characteristics such as vocal tremor, reduced loudness, hoarseness, or breathiness are not unique to Parkinson's disease. They can be triggered by numerous benign or non-neurological conditions, including upper respiratory infections, vocal fold nodules or polyps, laryngitis, asthma, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), normal age-related vocal changes (presbyphonia), or intense vocal fatigue.
- **Other Neurological Conditions:** Conditions such as Essential Tremor, Multiple System Atrophy (MSA), Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP), or stroke can generate speech alterations that overlap significantly with hypokinetic dysarthria.
- **Acoustic and Hardware Artifacts:** Voice recording quality is heavily influenced by the physical environment. Background room reverberation, ambient fan or traffic noise, microphone distance, clipping distortion, low-cost smartphone microphones, and lossy audio compression (such as MP3 or WebM codecs) can introduce acoustic artifacts that mimic pathological voice irregularities.
- **Risk of Classification Errors:** Statistical models inevitably produce both false positives (indicating elevated risk when no pathology exists) and false negatives (indicating low risk in individuals who do have neurological impairment).

Consequently, an acoustic screening tool cannot replace comprehensive clinical evaluations, neuroimaging, or specialized neurological testing.
